THE SETUP

The ledger is flat and unsorted. The same key appears on several records, so before trusting any line you must find every line mentioning it and apply a precedence rule:

  • The record with the latest as_of date wins.
  • If dates tie, source=AUDIT beats source=SELF-REPORT.
  • If still tied, the higher rev= number wins.

Position in the file is irrelevant — a superseded record may appear anywhere, including after the record that supersedes it. Identifiers are deliberately similar; a near-match is a wrong match.

THE QUESTION

Who is the custodian of the access code assigned to the building that contains the office of the staff member who leads project PRJ-7731?

WHY THIS KEEPS HAPPENING

The reasoning was never the problem. Resolving superseded records and breaking ties by source authority is the part everyone assumes is hard, and the model did it four times without error.

The failure was looking. Every link requires finding all matching lines in 300,000 characters. Get that 90% right and a five-link chain survives about 59% of the time. At 80% it is 33%. The chain does not break because reasoning degrades — it breaks because five consecutive perfect searches is a bet you lose.

One detail worth keeping: the model answered NOT IN CONTEXT rather than inventing a plausible custodian. Wrong, but wrong in the direction that does not hurt anyone. That distinction matters more than most benchmark scores.
